## Key Findings

- The purpose of PRISMA is to make systematic reviews *transparently* report why the review was
  done, what the authors did, and what they found — so users can judge trustworthiness and
  applicability.
- The standard is concrete: a 27-item checklist, an expanded item-by-item checklist, an abstract
  checklist, and revised flow diagrams. Rigor is operationalized as a reproducible reporting
  contract, not a vibe.
- PRISMA 2020 is explicitly a *reporting* guideline (it standardizes how a review is described),
  distinct from *conduct* guidance (Cochrane Handbook) — an important boundary for the meta-review.
- The update was driven by advances in methods to "identify, select, appraise, and synthesise
  studies," showing the standard itself is maintained and evolving.
