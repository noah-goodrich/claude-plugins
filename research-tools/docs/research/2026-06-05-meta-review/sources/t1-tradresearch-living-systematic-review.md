## Key Findings

- A living systematic review adopts a *continual* updating process — e.g., monthly searching followed
  by rapid incorporation of new evidence — directly attacking the slowness/staleness failure mode of
  conventional SRs (which can be years out of date by publication).
- It is not universally appropriate: it fits questions of high decision importance where new evidence
  is both likely and likely to change the conclusion. This is a scoping discipline, not a default.
- The honest tradeoff is explicit: "Considerable resources are required to support such an ongoing
  process." Currency is bought with sustained labor.
- This shows traditional methodology is self-correcting: it identified its own "too slow" failure mode
  and engineered a remedy (the COVID-19 living guidelines were the highest-profile application).

## Verified Quote(s)

**Location reference:** Section IV.1 (Introduction), Chapter IV "Updating a review," Cochrane Handbook
current version.

> 'living' systematic reviews that adopt a *continual* updating process, such as monthly searching
> followed by rapid incorporation of new evidence into the published review.

> Living systematic reviews are most likely to be appropriate for questions that are of high
> importance to decision makers, and for which new evidence is likely to be frequently published and
> to have an important impact on the review's findings

> Considerable resources are required to support such an ongoing process.
