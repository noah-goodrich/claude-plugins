## Key Findings

- Construct-validity critique: many anti-brainstorming studies "measure trivial creativity," e.g.
  "inventing product names for made up products," which doesn't reflect real innovation work.
- Procedure critique: study groups were typically "untrained in how to brainstorm, nor given an
  example of effective brainstorming to watch" — so they test bad brainstorming, not the method.
- Reframes the rules: "The intention of brainstorming is not to eliminate critique, but simply to
  postpone it" — a corrective to popular misreadings.
- Represents the ground-level practitioner view that the lab finding ("nominal beats real") is an
  artifact of artificial tasks and missing facilitation — directly contestable against Diehl &
  Stroebe and the nominal-group meta-analyses.
