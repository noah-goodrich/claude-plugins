## Key Findings

- Janis's groupthink model — wildly popular in management/practice — has WEAK empirical support. Park
  (2000) tested all 23 predictions of the full model; only 2 were confirmed.
- Lab studies cannot reliably reproduce groupthink conditions; field studies cannot show the specific
  model predicts outcomes. Critics call it "a compelling myth."
- This is a flagship INSTITUTIONAL-vs-GROUND divergence: the construct everyone "knows" is real is the one
  with the thinnest experimental backing. Cohesion — the supposed core driver — repeatedly fails to predict
  the symptoms.
- Design caution: do NOT build a tool's core thesis on groupthink as an established mechanism; treat it as a
  popular heuristic, not settled science. The robust group-process findings are production blocking and
  evaluation apprehension (Diehl & Stroebe), not groupthink.
