## Key Findings

- An independent expert re-analysis concluded essentially none of the A-Lab's 41 claimed "new" inorganic
  compounds were genuinely new — most were ordered versions of already-known disordered compounds.
- The core failure was that the AI could not handle compositional disorder / site mixing in X-ray
  diffraction: a different composition was wrongly assumed to be a new compound.
- The automated Rietveld refinement was described as "very bad, very beginner, completely novice human
  level," leading to misidentification of phases in mixtures.
- The Berkeley team (Ceder) defended the work as a demonstration of *what an autonomous lab can do*, not
  a claim of human-expert-quality characterization — reframing the milestone as a systems demo rather
  than a discovery.
