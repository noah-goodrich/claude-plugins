## Key Findings

- In a DYNAMIC design (each participant's ideas become stimuli for the next, mimicking cultural
  evolution), HIGH AI exposure INCREASED collective idea diversity vs. control (Cliff's Delta=0.31
  disclosed / 0.26 undisclosed, p=0.001) — the OPPOSITE of the homogenization finding.
- AI exposure did NOT change the creativity of individual ideas: F(4,19.86)=0.12, p=0.97. The
  authors summarize: "AI made ideas different, not better."
- Low AI exposure had no significant effect; only HIGH exposure moved diversity.
- The authors explicitly reconcile with prior work: passive exposure to SCATTERED AI ideas inside a
  human population can act as diversifying stimulus, whereas ACTIVE co-writing/anchoring (Doshi/
  Hauser) homogenizes. The design — not AI per se — drives the sign of the effect.
- Critical design lesson for an invention engine: how AI ideas are INJECTED (as stimulus to react
  against vs. as a draft to anchor on) may determine whether they widen or narrow the idea space.
