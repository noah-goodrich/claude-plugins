## Key Findings

- Osborn (1957) explicitly claimed group brainstorming yields "twice as many ideas" as working
  alone; this paper tests and refutes that specific claim.
- Across 22 prior experiments, 18 found nominal groups (individuals pooled) superior to real
  groups; only 4 (all 2-person) found no difference. None found real groups superior.
- Production blocking — only one person can speak at a time — "accounted for most of the
  productivity loss," not evaluation apprehension or free riding.
- Free riding was experimentally ruled out as a major cause; evaluation apprehension reduces
  individual output but does not interact with session type as a primary driver.
