## Key Findings

- Unadjusted, creativity training shows a moderate effect (0.53 SDs); after publication-bias
  adjustment the estimate collapses to 0.29–0.32 SDs.
- "Converging evidence consistent with strong publication bias" casts doubt on prior optimistic
  meta-analyses (Scott et al. 2004 reported d=0.64; some fields reported d up to 1.02).
- High prevalence of methodological shortcomings, with "little signs of methodological improvement
  over time" — the evidence base for creativity methods is weak and not getting better.
- Even the adjusted ~0.3 SD effect is broadly in line with education/psychology intervention
  benchmarks, so the methods are not worthless — just far less impressive than folklore claims.
