## Key Findings

- GRADE rates a *body* of evidence on four certainty levels — high, moderate, low, very low —
  expressing confidence that the true effect is close to the estimate, rather than ranking single
  study designs.
- RCTs start at high certainty and non-randomized studies start at low — but the starting point is
  only a starting point.
- Five domains can *downgrade* certainty: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision,
  and publication bias. This is the mechanism that prevents naive "RCT = truth" reasoning.
- Three criteria can *upgrade* observational evidence: large magnitude of effect, dose-response
  gradient, and plausible residual confounding that would only strengthen the observed effect. This
  is what the crude "hierarchy of designs" misses — GRADE is a hierarchy of *certainty*, not a fixed
  ladder of study types.
