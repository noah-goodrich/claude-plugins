## Key Findings

- All four tools (Scite, Elicit, Consensus, Scopus AI) returned REAL citations, but real citations do not
  guarantee the citation matches the argument generated — a distinct failure mode from fabricated references.
- Tools sometimes conclude from a paper's introductory/abstract generalities rather than its specific findings,
  and sometimes quote secondary sources, introducing bias.
- Scopus AI was the worst: instances where sources did not support the arguments, and one case of near
  word-for-word copying from an abstract (plagiarism risk).
- Coverage is shallow: Scite/Consensus ~10 citations, Scopus 5-8, Elicit 4 (8 for Plus). For new/nuanced topics,
  traditional databases (Google Scholar) may be more reliable.
- Institutional bottom line: use as a starting point, not a replacement for a thorough literature review.
