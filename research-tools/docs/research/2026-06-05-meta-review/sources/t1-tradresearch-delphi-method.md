## Key Findings

- The Delphi method is a structured, iterative, anonymous expert-elicitation technique: experts
  answer questionnaires over two or more rounds, with a facilitator feeding back an anonymized summary
  (plus reasons) between rounds.
- The mechanism that makes it work: anonymity "prevents the authority, personality, or reputation of
  some participants from dominating others" and minimizes bandwagon/halo effects — it engineers around
  the social-pressure failure modes of face-to-face panels.
- Its honest failure mode is garbage-in: "If panelists are misinformed about a topic, the use of
  Delphi may only add confidence to their ignorance." Consensus is not correctness.
- Track record is candidly "mixed" — a useful corrective to treating any consensus method as
  inherently rigorous. This mirrors the broader theme: structure mitigates specific biases but cannot
  manufacture truth from bad inputs.

> The experts answer questionnaires in two or more rounds. After each round, a facilitator provides an
> anonymised summary of the experts' forecasts from the previous round as well as the reasons they
> provided for their judgments.

> This prevents the authority, personality, or reputation of some participants from dominating others
> in the process [...] minimizes the 'bandwagon effect' or 'halo effect', allows free expression of
> opinions, encourages open critique.

> If panelists are misinformed about a topic, the use of Delphi may only add confidence to their
