## Key Findings

- GMA, developed by astrophysicist Fritz Zwicky, structures a problem into parameters and
  conditions in a "Zwicky box," then enumerates all configurations in the problem space.
- Cross-Consistency Assessment (CCA) prunes the combinatorial explosion: a field of ~100,000
  formal configurations needs only a few hundred pairwise consistency judgments to yield a
  tractable "solution space" — the key scalability trick versus naive brute force.
- The method is explicitly non-quantified and judgment-based; it relies on logical and empirical
  (not initially normative) pairwise judgments, which is both its strength and its subjectivity.
- Ritchey reports group-variance trials: identical fields given to different groups produced
  diverging CCA results — an honest admission that outputs depend on who fills the matrix.
