## Key Findings

- Access to AI ideas causally raised stories' rated creativity, writing quality, and enjoyability;
  the uplift was largest for writers with lower inherent creativity (it leveled them up to high-
  creativity peers).
- AI-assisted stories were measurably MORE similar to each other than human-only stories
  (~10.7% increase in pairwise similarity for the one-idea condition), using cosine similarity of
  text embeddings ×100.
- Authors frame this as a "social dilemma": each writer is individually better off, but the
  collective pool of novel content narrows — individual incentive diverges from collective good.
- Effect sizes for the five-AI-idea condition among less-creative writers: +10.7% novelty,
  +11.5% usefulness, +26.6% "better written," +15.2% "less boring."
- Writers did not perceive their own improvement; external evaluators consistently did — a
  self-assessment gap.

## Verified Quote(s)

**Location reference:** Abstract (full), as rendered on PMC PMC11244532 and Science Advances.

> Creativity is core to being human. Generative artificial intelligence (AI)—including powerful
> large language models (LLMs)—holds promise for humans to be more creative by offering new ideas,
> or less creative by anchoring on generative AI ideas. We study the causal impact of generative AI
> ideas on the production of short stories in an online experiment where some writers obtained story
> ideas from an LLM. We find that access to generative AI ideas causes stories to be evaluated as
> more creative, better written, and more enjoyable, especially among less creative writers.
> However, generative AI–enabled stories are more similar to each other than stories by humans
> alone. These results point to an increase in individual creativity at the risk of losing
> collective novelty.

> This dynamic resembles a social dilemma: With generative AI, writers are individually better off,
> but collectively a narrower scope of novel content is produced.
