## Key Findings

- The 2015 Reproducibility Project (Nosek et al.) replicated 100 psychology studies; of 97 with
  original significant effects, only 36% replicated at p < 0.05, with effect sizes roughly half the
  originals. This is the empirical foundation of the "replication crisis."
- The crisis is defined broadly as "widespread failures to reproduce published scientific results,"
  extending beyond psychology into medicine, economics, and other fields.
- Preregistration — registering hypotheses, methods, and analyses before data collection — is the
  headline structural remedy, because it lets the p-value be computed correctly and constrains
  questionable research practices (p-hacking, optional stopping, HARKing).
- The reform narrative is institutionally backed (Center for Open Science; TOP guidelines adopted by
  500+ journals), but the article underrepresents credible skeptics — a known contested zone.
