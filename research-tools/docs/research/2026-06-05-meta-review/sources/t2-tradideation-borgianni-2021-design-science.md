## Key Findings

- The reliability of the contradiction matrix "is often questioned" and "an agreement on a solid
  and reliable procedure for the selection of [Inventive Principles] is far from being reached."
- Statistical testing rejects a strictly deterministic mapping of principles to contradiction
  types, but only narrowly (random-distribution probability 5.3%, near the threshold).
- For mechanical problems specifically, "the distribution of IPs across kinds of contradictions
  appears as considerably random" — i.e., the matrix barely beats chance in TRIZ's original domain.
- Recommends a domain-oriented workflow: determine the problem domain first, then define the
  contradiction — implying the canonical matrix alone is insufficient.

## Inclusion Decision

**Decision:** Core
**Rationale:** Authority + Relevance + Honesty. This is the rigorous academic counterweight to
TRIZ marketing: it shows the contradiction matrix — TRIZ's most famous tool — has questionable
reliability and behaves near-randomly in its home domain. Essential for an honest "invention
engine" assessment.

**Redundancy check:** Adds quantitative distributional evidence not present in the more
qualitative Spreafico & Russo survey; complements rather than duplicates.
