## Key Findings

- "Current AI models understand essentially nothing about chemistry. They are purely statistical and
  correlative in nature" — the central epistemic limit of AI hypothesis generators: they pattern-match,
  they don't reason about mechanism.
- AI models are "black boxes," so "one should not blindly trust their results and draw conclusions from
  them" — opacity is a first-order risk for hypothesis-generation systems, not a footnote.
- The danger is over-interpretation: assuming the features an AI weights actually *cause* the desired
  activity, when they may be spurious correlations (the "classify a radio as a car" failure mode).
- The corrective is mandatory: "A plausibility check based on a sound scientific rationale is of critical
  importance," plus experimental validation — exactly the human-in-the-loop step the celebratory
  announcements (Robin, Co-Scientist) rely on but underemphasize.
