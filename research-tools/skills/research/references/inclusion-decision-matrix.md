# Inclusion/Exclusion Decision Matrix

The keep/throw decision is the most consequential step in the research pipeline. It determines
what evidence shapes the final analysis and recommendations. Every decision must be documented
and defensible.

---

## Decision Inputs

The decision combines THREE inputs, each evaluated independently:

### Input 1: Composite Credibility Score
From the 10-dimension evaluation rubric.

| Score range | Signal |
|-------------|--------|
| 7.0 – 10.0 | Strong credibility — default include |
| 5.0 – 6.9 | Moderate credibility — include if other factors favor it |
| 3.0 – 4.9 | Weak credibility — default exclude unless override applies |
| 0.0 – 2.9 | Very weak — exclude; document why it was evaluated at all |

### Input 2: Evidence Level
From the 9-level evidence hierarchy.

| Level | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| 1–3 | High-rigor evidence — weight heavily in synthesis |
| 4–6 | Moderate-rigor evidence — useful for context and triangulation |
| 7–9 | Low-rigor evidence — useful for perspective and hypothesis generation |

### Input 3: Contextual Factors
Five additional factors that modify the decision:

| Factor | Keep signal | Throw signal |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| **Redundancy** | Adds new information, methodology, or perspective not already covered | Duplicates what a stronger-scoring source already provides |
| **Perspective diversity** | Represents a source category (academic, institutional, practitioner, boots-on-the-ground, contrarian) underrepresented in this topic area | Echoes the majority view without adding nuance |
| **Contextual fit** | Addresses the target audience, geography, economic conditions, or demographics relevant to the research | Wrong context (e.g., corporate finance advice applied to households; developing-country data applied to US context) |
| **Actionability** | Leads to a concrete insight, recommendation, or design decision | Purely descriptive or theoretical with no practical takeaway |
| **Unique insight** | Contains a specific finding, framework, or perspective not found elsewhere in the research corpus | Generic advice available from dozens of sources |

---

## Decision Rules

Work through these rules IN ORDER. Stop at the first rule that matches.

### Rule 1: Strong Include
**Condition:** Composite score ≥ 7.0 AND not redundant
**Decision:** **Core source** — cite in analysis and recommendations
**Action:** Mark as "Core" on source card

### Rule 2: Diversity Include
**Condition:** Source represents the ONLY voice from an important perspective category in this
topic area, regardless of score
**Decision:** **Supporting source** — include with caveats about credibility
**Action:** Mark as "Supporting (diversity override)" on source card. Note which perspective
category it fills and why no stronger source exists for that perspective.

### Rule 3: Unique Insight Include
**Condition:** Composite score ≥ 5.0 AND contains a specific finding or framework not found
in any other evaluated source
**Decision:** **Supporting source** — include for the unique contribution
**Action:** Mark as "Supporting (unique insight)" on source card. Name the specific insight.

### Rule 4: Moderate Include
**Condition:** Composite score 5.0–6.9 AND at least 2 of the 5 contextual factors favor
inclusion
**Decision:** **Supporting source** — provides context, noted in research section
**Action:** Mark as "Supporting" on source card. Note which contextual factors favored
inclusion.

### Rule 5: Weak Exclude
**Condition:** Composite score < 5.0 AND redundant AND no unique perspective
**Decision:** **Excluded**
**Action:** Mark as "Excluded" on source card with specific factors.

### Rule 6: Default Exclude
**Condition:** None of the above rules matched (typically: moderate score but redundant and
no unique contribution)
**Decision:** **Excluded**
**Action:** Mark as "Excluded (superseded by [name stronger source])" on source card.

---

## Override Protocol

Sometimes the rules above produce a wrong answer. Overrides exist for this.

**When to override:**
- A low-scoring source is the ONLY firsthand account of a widely-discussed phenomenon
- A high-scoring source has a fatal flaw not captured by the rubric (e.g., retracted paper,
  author later discredited)
- A source's value is in what it reveals about the DISCOURSE rather than its factual content
  (e.g., a popular but misleading article that shaped public understanding — include to
  explain the misconception, not to support the claim)

**Override documentation requirements:**
1. State the rule that would have applied without the override
2. State the specific reason for overriding
3. State what role this source will play in the final document (context? counterexample?
   historical artifact?)

## Perspective Balance Check

After all inclusion/exclusion decisions are made for a topic area, check the distribution
across the 5 source categories:

1. **Academic** — peer-reviewed research, university publications
2. **Institutional** — government agencies, professional bodies, established financial orgs
3. **Practitioner** — financial advisors, coaches, tool builders, counselors
4. **Boots-on-the-ground** — forum discussions, personal blogs, community experience
5. **Contrarian** — voices that challenge the mainstream consensus

**Minimum standard:** At least 3 of the 5 categories should be represented among included
sources for each major topic area.

**If a category is missing:** Document why. Is it because no credible sources exist in that
category for this topic? Or because the search strategy didn't find them? If the latter,
do more searching.

---

## Documentation Checklist

For the methodology section of the final document:

- [ ] Total sources discovered (raw search results)
- [ ] Total sources evaluated (cards completed)
- [ ] Total sources included (Core + Supporting)
- [ ] Total sources excluded (with breakdown by exclusion reason)
- [ ] Distribution by evidence level (how many at each of the 9 levels)
- [ ] Distribution by source category (academic/institutional/practitioner/boots/contrarian)
- [ ] Distribution by credibility score tier (7+, 5-6.9, 3-4.9, <3)
- [ ] Any overrides applied (count and summary)
