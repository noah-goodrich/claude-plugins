# Research Document Template

Use this structure for the final research deliverable. The ordering is intentional — readers see
the conclusions first, then can dig as deep as they want. A busy reader stops after the summary.
A curious reader reads the analysis. A skeptical reader checks the methodology and bibliography.

---

```markdown
# [Research Title]

*Conducted: [date range]*
*Methodology: deep-research v0.1.0*

---

## 1. Recommendations

[Actionable bullet points. What should the reader DO based on this research?

Each recommendation should:
- Start with a verb (Build, Use, Avoid, Adopt, Test, etc.)
- Include WHY in one clause
- Reference which analysis section backs it up
- Be understandable without reading anything else in the document

Group recommendations by audience if there are multiple stakeholders.

Target: 5-15 recommendations. More than 15 means the research scope was too broad or the
recommendations aren't specific enough.]

---

## 2. Summary

[2-3 pages maximum. The "tired dad at 4am" version.

Write this LAST but place it SECOND. It should:
- Explain what was researched and why
- State the 3-5 most important findings in plain language
- Note where experts agree, where they disagree, and what surprised you
- Define any terms a non-expert would need
- Use concrete examples, not abstractions
- End with "the one thing to remember" — if the reader retains only one insight]

---

## 3. [Domain-Specific Framework Section]

[Title this section based on the research domain. For household finance: "Household Financial
Archetypes." For photography: "Photographic Scoring Dimensions." For software: "Architecture
Patterns."

This section presents any framework, typology, or model that emerged from the research.

Include:
- The framework itself (dimensions, categories, archetypes — whatever fits)
- Evidence backing each component (cite specific sources with credibility scores)
- How this framework compares to existing ones in the literature
- Limitations and boundary conditions
- How to apply the framework in practice]

---

## 4. Analysis

[Deep dive organized by theme/topic area.

For each theme:
- State the research question for this theme
- Present findings across source categories (what academics say, what institutions
  recommend, what practitioners report, what real people experience)
- Note consensus zones (most sources agree)
- Note contested zones (credible sources disagree — present both sides fairly)
- Note gaps (important questions nobody is answering well)
- Highlight the delta between institutional advice and boots-on-the-ground reality

Use this format for each theme:

### [Theme Name]

**Research question:** [specific question this theme addresses]

**What the evidence says:** [synthesis across sources]

**Where sources agree:** [consensus findings]

**Where sources disagree:** [contested areas with strongest arguments on each side]

**What's missing:** [gaps in the evidence base]

**Institutional vs. ground truth:** [how big-name advice compares to lived experience]]

---

## 5. Research

[Full findings organized by topic area with source citations.

This is the raw material that feeds the Analysis section. Include:
- Every key finding from every included source
- Source citation with credibility score in brackets, e.g., [Author, Score: 7.8]
- Evidence level in brackets, e.g., [Level 3: Observational study]
- Direct quotes where the source's own words are more precise than paraphrase

Organize by the topic areas defined in the research design phase.]

---

## 6. Methodology

[See methodology-section-template.md for the detailed structure.

This section answers: "How did you decide what to include and exclude?"
It's the "show your work" section.]

---

## 7. Bibliography

[All sources, organized by topic area, with:
- Full citation
- Composite credibility score
- Evidence level
- Inclusion decision (Core / Supporting)
- One-line summary of the source's contribution

Example:
> **Klontz, B. et al.** "Money Beliefs and Financial Behaviors." *Journal of Financial
> Therapy.* 2011. Score: 8.2 | Level 3 | Core.
> Foundational research on money scripts — the unconscious beliefs about money formed in
> childhood that drive adult financial behavior.

Excluded sources go in a separate subsection with exclusion reason.]
```

---

## Language Guide

Apply these rules throughout the document:

1. **ELI10** — Explain Like I'm 10. Not condescending, just clear. Replace jargon with plain
   words. If you must use a technical term, define it inline on first use.

2. **Concrete > abstract.** Not "financial behaviors vary significantly across demographics."
   Instead: "A family making $60K/year in Salt Lake City handles money differently than a
   single 25-year-old making $120K in San Francisco — and advice that works for one can
   actively hurt the other."

3. **Define on first use.** Example: "**Zero-based budgeting** (giving every dollar a job
   before the month starts, so your income minus your planned spending equals zero)..."

4. **Show the tension.** The most interesting finding is usually WHERE experts disagree or
   where institutional advice diverges from reality. Lead with tension, not consensus.

5. **No orphaned claims.** Every factual statement should have a citation. Opinions and
   synthesis should be clearly marked as such.
